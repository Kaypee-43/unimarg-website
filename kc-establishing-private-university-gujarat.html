How to establish a private university in Gujarat.

Setting up a private university in Gujarat runs on a fixed annual cycle. An eligible sponsoring body applies by the end of October with a detailed project report and a ten lakh rupee fee. A state scrutiny committee studies the proposal, visits the site and reports back. If the government is satisfied it issues a Letter of Intent, and once those conditions are met the university's name is added to the schedule of the state Act by legislation.

By Satya Prakash Tiwary, Founding Partner, UniMarg Last reviewed September 2026 12 min read

Two conditions decide whether you can apply at all, and both sit right at the start. The sponsoring body needs seven years of experience running higher education institutions, and it needs at least five acres of land.

The law behind it

Two documents govern this work. The Gujarat Private Universities Act, 2009 creates the framework. The Gujarat Private Universities Rules, 2020 supply the numbers: the eligibility conditions, the application fee, the endowment amounts, the filing deadline and the forms. You can download both below.

Most guidance available online quotes only the Act, which is why so much of it stays vague about money and dates. The figures live in the Rules.

Above the state framework sits the University Grants Commission. Its regulations for private universities require that each one is created by a state Act, that it operates as a single unitary university, and that any off campus centre waits five years and then needs clearance from both the UGC and the state government.

Approval comes from the assembly, not from a licence

This is the part promoters find most surprising, and it changes how a project should be planned.

Every private university in Gujarat exists because its name appears in a schedule attached to the 2009 Act. Adding a name to that schedule means amending the Act, which means a Bill passed in the state assembly. Approvals are usually cleared in batches.

A private university in Gujarat is not licensed. It is legislated.

Your project does not finish with an officer's signature. It finishes when the assembly sits. That is not a delay anyone can push through with follow up, and it is the reason we tell promoters that being ready before the filing window matters far more than moving quickly after it.

Who is allowed to apply

An individual cannot apply. Neither can a partnership firm or an ordinary private limited company.

The application has to come from a sponsoring body, and that means one of four things:

  • a society registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860
  • a public trust registered under the Gujarat Public Trusts Act, 1950
  • a company registered under Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013
  • a society or trust registered under the law of any other state in India

That last one is often missed. A trust registered outside Gujarat is perfectly eligible to sponsor a university here, which matters for groups expanding into the state from elsewhere.

The seven year condition

Beyond the legal form, the sponsoring body must have at least seven years of experience running higher education institutions.

This single requirement ends more conversations than anything else in the process. A promoter may have the land, the capital, a sound academic plan and genuine commitment, and still not be able to file. The condition attaches to the body making the application, and it cannot be arranged at the last minute.

Where a promoter does not meet it, there are two workable routes. The first is to establish or acquire an institution and build the record over time, which turns a university project into a longer sequence with a college at the front of it. The second is to bring the project through a sponsoring body that already qualifies. Both are ordinary decisions in this sector, but they have to be taken early, because the shape of everything else depends on which one you choose.

Any honest feasibility exercise tests this before it tests the market.

Land and buildings

Five acres is the minimum. It is not the answer to how much land you need.

The application also asks for the minimum land required for each faculty or discipline under existing norms. A university offering engineering, pharmacy or medical programmes will be held to the council norms for those subjects, and those alone usually run well beyond five acres.

Alongside area, the file has to show:

  • ownership of the land, or a clear plan to acquire it and develop the campus in phases
  • non agricultural permission, with agricultural and non agricultural holdings listed separately, and survey details by location
  • approved building plans with building use permissions attached
  • faculty wise tables of intake, construction area and land area, covering academic and residential buildings separately
  • evidence and photographs for the library, laboratories, computer facilities, hostels for boys and girls, staff quarters, sports ground, gymnasium, canteen, auditorium, transport and infirmary

Land still standing in a promoter's personal name at the time of filing is a common and entirely avoidable problem. So is a campus designed without reference to the norms for the programmes it is meant to house. Both are cheap to fix a year early and expensive to fix in October.

What it costs

Application fee

Ten lakh rupees, non refundable, paid by demand draft from a nationalised bank in favour of the Commissioner of Higher Education, Gujarat State, Gandhinagar.

If you miss October and file under the December extension, a second fee of the same amount becomes payable. A missed window therefore costs twenty lakh in fees alone, and the extension is available only where you can show the delay was genuinely outside your control.

Endowment fund

There is no single figure. The amount is tied to what you plan to invest in the university over its first five years, counting both recurring and non recurring expenditure.

Investment planned for the first five yearsEndowment fund
Below 10 crore1 crore
10 crore to 50 crore3 crore
Above 50 crore5 crore

The money goes to Gujarat State Financial Services as a corporate deposit and earns interest at prevailing rates.

What it is not is working capital. The state holds it as a security deposit and can forfeit part or all of it if the university breaks the rules it operates under. Only the interest can be spent, and only on developing infrastructure, never on running costs. The principal stays where it is until the university is wound up.

Two things follow. The slab is decided by a figure you write in your own project report, so an ambitious five year capital plan can move the requirement by two crore or four crore. And the endowment should be modelled as money that leaves the business, not as a reserve you can draw on later.

The sequence, step by step

In the order it actually happens. The most expensive mistake is starting at step four.

  1. Check eligibility. Test the sponsoring body first: seven years of higher education operations, five acres of land, and one of the four permitted legal forms. If it fails, this is a restructuring question before it is an application question, and the answer changes the timeline by years.
  2. Test the idea. Work out which disciplines, which programmes go first, what enrolment is realistic year by year, what the catchment supports and who already serves it. The application asks whether the university is built around one domain or several, and what gap in education its courses fill.
  3. Settle land and money. Get the land into the sponsoring body's name with the permissions in place. Fix the phased capital plan, because the five year investment figure decides the endowment slab. Cost estimates are needed at three levels of development, not one.
  4. Build the project report. The report runs to more than twenty separate heads, from the sponsoring body's audited accounts to the fee structure, scholarship policy, faculty plan, campus development phasing and the case for why this university is needed. The application form and the supporting undertakings sit alongside it.
  5. File by the end of October. The application goes to the state government with the demand draft. December filing is possible, but only on proven cause and at a second fee.
  6. Scrutiny committee and site visit. The committee is chaired by the Principal Secretary of the Education Department, with the Director of Technical Education, a vice chancellor nominated by the state, and the Commissioner or Director of Higher Education as member secretary. You present, you answer its questions, and at least two of its representatives visit the proposed campus. The committee then reports, as far as possible, within three months.
  7. Letter of Intent. On the report, the government may issue a Letter of Intent, ask for more detail, or reject the proposal. The Letter sets the conditions to be met, including the endowment deposit. It also fixes the fields the university may operate in, so anything outside it needs separate approval later.
  8. Legislation. Once satisfied that the conditions are met, the government brings a Bill adding the university, with its name, location and jurisdiction, to the schedule. On enactment the university exists as a body corporate.
  9. Open properly. First statutes within six months. Leadership and faculty appointed, academic bodies constituted, admissions and examination systems running, UGC formalities completed and council approvals secured for the programmes that need them. NAAC accreditation within five years.

What you are committing to

The application carries a set of undertakings given on affidavit. They are worth reading before the project report is finalised rather than after, because several of them close off options a promoter may assume are still open.

  • Courses will carry the sanction or approval of the relevant regulatory body, including courses that are still only proposed
  • The university will follow directions issued by the Education Department
  • Books and journals worth at least ten lakh rupees at the start, and a commitment to spend at least thirty lakh on the library and its networking within three years, or the regulator's norms if those are higher
  • Preference to bonafide residents of Gujarat in employment
  • Full disclosure of foreign funding, including whether it falls under the Foreign Contribution Regulation Act
  • Prior approval before collaborating with other universities, research institutions, industry or organisations, in India or abroad
  • Prior approval before offering joint degrees with another institution, and that institution must be properly accredited in its own country
  • The university will not be dissolved for fifteen years from its establishment

The last one deserves a second look. It is a fifteen year commitment entered into at application stage, before a single student has been admitted.

Life after establishment

Being established changes what the institution is. It does not remove the conditions it was built under, and several of them shape decisions promoters often think are still theirs to make.

The university cannot affiliate colleges

Universities under the Act are non affiliating. They cannot take other colleges or institutions under their umbrella for the purpose of awarding degrees, and the UGC separately requires a private university to be unitary. A promoter whose plan assumes that university status lets them bring an existing network of colleges under one name has the wrong plan, and will find out at committee stage. What a university can do is set up and run its own constituent colleges and centres within Gujarat.

There are no government grants

Grant in aid and other financial assistance from the state or central government are ruled out. The model has to work on fees, endowment income, consultancy revenue and what the sponsoring body itself puts in.

Admissions and fees are regulated

Admission is strictly on merit. The state's existing provisions on admissions and fee structure apply, and professional programmes fall under the separate state legislation governing admission and fee fixation for technical and medical institutions. Fee freedom is narrower than most promoters expect, and it is better to know that while the financial model is still being built.

NAAC accreditation is compulsory

Within five years of establishment the university has to obtain NAAC accreditation, along with any other body connected to the courses it runs, tell the state government what grade it received, and renew that accreditation over time.

Continuing oversight

The university stays bound by the norms of the central regulating bodies. The state can assess teaching, examination and research standards and issue directions that have to be complied with immediately. Details of students, faculty, staff, infrastructure, finances, assets and liabilities are reported to the government every year. Where the state finds a breach, financial mismanagement or maladministration, it can issue a show cause notice and ultimately appoint an administrator to run the university.

Winding up is slow by design

A sponsoring body that wants to dissolve the university gives notice, a review committee is formed, and the dissolution takes effect only after the last batches of students on regular courses have finished and received their degrees.

How long it takes

There is no guaranteed timeline, and anyone quoting one as a certainty is guessing. What can be stated with confidence are the three clocks that govern the calendar.

  • The filing deadline. End of October each year, extendable to end of December only on proven cause and at a second fee. Miss it and the project waits a full cycle.
  • The scrutiny committee. Reports back to government, as far as possible, within three months of the proposal reaching it. This is the only period the law itself commits to.
  • The assembly session. Adding the university to the schedule needs an Act, so it happens when the assembly sits, usually with several universities taken together.

Everything between those points depends on how complete the file is rather than on how fast the administration moves. A project that arrives in October with a consistent report, land settled, permissions in hand and the endowment arranged will move through the cycle. A project that arrives with gaps spends its time answering queries and then meets the next window instead.

So the useful planning question is not how many months this takes. It is which October you are filing for, and what has to be true by then.

Where projects go wrong

These are the recurring causes we see, and nearly all of them are settled long before anything is submitted.

  • The seven year condition is not met. The most common disqualifier by a distance. It cannot be fixed at application stage, which is why it is the first thing to establish about any project.
  • Land is not application ready. Held in the wrong name, short of five acres, short of the discipline norms, or missing non agricultural and building use permissions.
  • The endowment was estimated from a moving number. The five year capital plan shifts during preparation and the deposit turns out to be under provisioned at Letter of Intent stage.
  • October is missed. Usually because the project report was treated as the last task rather than the organising one.
  • The plan assumes affiliation. A group of existing colleges cannot be affiliated to the new university.
  • The financial model assumes grants. That line has to come out of the projection entirely.
  • Programme approvals are assumed rather than secured. The undertaking covers proposed courses too, not only the ones already running.
  • The site visit is treated as a formality. What the report describes should be visible on the ground when the committee arrives.

Questions

Frequently asked questions.

Which law governs private universities in Gujarat?

The Gujarat Private Universities Act, 2009, together with the Gujarat Private Universities Rules, 2020. The Act creates the framework and the Rules carry the conditions, the fee and the deadline.

Who can apply to establish a private university in Gujarat?

Only a sponsoring body. That means a society registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860, a public trust registered under the Gujarat Public Trusts Act, 1950, a company registered under Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013, or a society or trust registered under the law of any other state in India. An individual, a partnership firm or an ordinary private limited company cannot apply.

Can a first time promoter with no existing college start a university in Gujarat?

Not directly. The sponsoring body must have at least seven years of experience running higher education institutions. A promoter without that record usually has to build or acquire an institution first, or bring the project through a sponsoring body that already qualifies.

How much land is needed for a private university in Gujarat?

Five acres is the minimum to be eligible to apply. The application also asks for the minimum land required for each faculty or discipline under existing norms, so a university offering engineering, pharmacy or medical programmes will normally need considerably more.

What is the application fee?

Ten lakh rupees, non refundable, paid by demand draft from a nationalised bank in favour of the Commissioner of Higher Education, Gujarat State, Gandhinagar.

How much is the endowment fund?

It depends on what you plan to invest in the first five years. One crore rupees if that investment is below ten crore, three crore if it falls between ten and fifty crore, and five crore above fifty crore. The money is deposited with Gujarat State Financial Services and stays there until the university is wound up.

Is there a deadline to apply?

Yes. Applications are made by the end of October each year. A late application can be accepted up to the end of December, but only if you can show the delay was outside your control, and a second fee of the same amount becomes payable.

Can a private university in Gujarat affiliate colleges?

No. Universities under the Act are non affiliating. They cannot take other colleges or institutions under their umbrella for the purpose of awarding degrees. A university can, however, set up and run its own constituent colleges and centres.

Do private universities in Gujarat get government grants?

No. The Act rules out grant in aid and any other financial assistance from the state or central government. The financial model has to work on fees, endowment income and the sponsoring body's own resources.

Is NAAC accreditation compulsory?

Yes. The university has to obtain NAAC accreditation within five years of being established, inform the state government of the grade it receives, and renew that accreditation over time.

How long does the whole process take?

There is no guaranteed timeline. Two dates shape the calendar: the October filing deadline, and the assembly session at which the Bill adding your university to the schedule is passed. The only fixed period is that the scrutiny committee reports back, as far as possible, within three months.

What happens after the university is established?

The first statutes go to the government within six months. After that the university has to follow the norms of the central regulating bodies, admit students on merit, work within the state rules on admissions and fees, report its particulars to the government every year, and obtain NAAC accreditation within five years.

How to establish a private university in Rajasthan.

Rajasthan sets a clear, front-loaded bar, and it creates each private university by its own Act. An eligible sponsoring body applies to the State Government with a project report, a committee examines it, and if the Government is satisfied it issues a Letter of Intent. The sponsoring body then has to put real assets in place, a minimum of thirty acres of land, ten thousand square metres of covered space and an endowment fund, before the university is finally established by a dedicated Act passed for it by the State Legislature. Unlike Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan has kept these fixed minimums, so the capacity to commit land and capital early is part of the test.

By UniMarg Higher Education Consultancy Reviewed by the UniMarg advisory team, October 2026 11 min read

Rajasthan has long been one of the more active states for private universities, and its route has two features that catch promoters out. The conditions that decide a project, land, covered space and an endowment fund, have to be met in full before the university comes into existence, and the final step is not an administrative notification but a dedicated Act of the State Legislature for that specific university. A promoter who understands both plans the assets and the file together, rather than treating the approval as a licence to be chased.

The law behind it

Rajasthan first legislated a general framework, the Rajasthan Private Universities Act, 2005. That general Act was then repealed, with effect from 5 July 2007, by the Rajasthan Private Universities (Repeal) Act, 2007. This matters, because guidance that still describes the 2005 Act as the governing law is out of date.

Since the repeal, the operative framework is the Government of Rajasthan's Guidelines for the Establishment of Private Universities by Separate Act, issued on 26 July 2007. As the title says, each private university is established by its own separate Act of the State Legislature. The guidelines set out who may sponsor a university, the conditions that attach to a Letter of Intent, and the examination and verification process that precedes the Act. This is why the state's statute book carries a distinct Act for each private university rather than a single schedule of names.

Above the state framework sits the University Grants Commission. Its regulations for private universities require that each is created by a state legislative route, operates as a single unitary university, and that any off campus centre is subject to separate clearance. A private university in Rajasthan is also self-financed, so it cannot look to the State Government for grants.

One caution before the detail. The guidelines date from 2007, and some figures and fees are set by rule or departmental order rather than fixed permanently. Every amount on this page should be confirmed against the current position with the Department of College Education before it is relied on. The structure of the route, an application, a Letter of Intent, a compliance year and then an Act, has been stable.

Who is allowed to apply

An individual cannot apply, and neither can a partnership firm. The application has to come from a sponsoring body, and under clause 1 of the guidelines that means one of the following:

  • a society registered under the Rajasthan Societies Registration Act, 1958, or the Societies Registration Act, 1860
  • a public trust registered under the Rajasthan Public Trusts Act, 1959, or the Indian Trusts Act, 1882, or the equivalent law of another state or union territory
  • a company registered under Section 25 of the Companies Act, 1956, now Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013

The project report has to carry the sponsoring body's registration documents, its objectives, its financial resources and its audited accounts, among a long list of required contents in clause 3. There is no formal bar of prior educational experience, but a sponsoring body with no financial track record will struggle to build a credible file.

Land and buildings

This is where Rajasthan differs most from the states that have relaxed their rules. The Letter of Intent conditions in clause 9 require the sponsoring body to own a minimum of thirty acres of contiguous land and to construct a minimum of ten thousand square metres of covered space for administration and academic purposes.

These are floors, not ceilings. Where the UGC, AICTE or another council sets higher land or infrastructure norms for the programmes an institution intends to run, those norms apply, so a university planning engineering, pharmacy or medical programmes will need the space those disciplines require on top of the basic minimum. The guidelines also set minimum early investment in learning resources and equipment, covered below.

The most common and most avoidable problem here is land that is not application ready: held in a promoter's personal name rather than the sponsoring body's, short of the thirty acres, not contiguous, or without clear ownership papers. Each of these is far cheaper to fix a year early than at the compliance stage, when the clock on the Letter of Intent is already running.

What it costs

Application fee

The proposal is filed with a demand draft of one lakh rupees in favour of the Commissioner, College Education, Rajasthan, Jaipur, under clause 2 of the guidelines. Because such amounts can be revised by order, the current figure should be confirmed with the Department of College Education.

The endowment fund

Under clause 9 of the guidelines the sponsoring body must create an endowment fund, and the amount depends on where the university is located.

Location of the universityEndowment fund
At least 15 km from the municipal limits of a Divisional Headquarters city, or at least 10 km from other citiesRs 1 crore
In a scheduled tribal area, and meeting that distance from a cityRs 75 lakh
All other casesRs 2 crore

The logic is deliberate: the state asks for less where a university helps take higher education further from the main cities, and most where it sits in or near them. The fund is not a fee. It is a security the sponsoring body creates and keeps invested, so in the financial model it is capital locked away rather than a one-time payment, which is a materially different commitment.

Early investment in resources and equipment

Clause 9 also requires undertakings on early spend: books and journals worth at least ten lakh rupees, with at least fifty lakh rupees invested within three years; and equipment and infrastructure worth at least twenty lakh rupees, rising to at least one crore rupees within five years. Where a regulator's norm is higher, that norm applies.

Rajasthan asks a promoter to put the assets in place first, and be established by an Act second.

The land, the covered space and the endowment are conditions of the Letter of Intent, met before the university exists. A plan that plans to raise them afterward has the sequence backwards.

The sequence, step by step

In the order it actually happens. The most expensive mistake is starting in the middle, with land or buildings, before the sponsoring body and the file are ready.

  1. Form and test the sponsoring body. Confirm it is one of the permitted registered entities, and that it can show audited accounts and genuine financial capacity. This is a structuring question before it is an application question.
  2. Build the project report. Prepare the full project report clause 3 requires: the sponsoring body's registration and finances, the campus location, land and infrastructure, the programmes and faculties proposed with enrolment targets, the fee structure, the admission and staff systems, and the justification for the university.
  3. File the proposal with the State Government. The application, the project report and the one lakh rupee demand draft go to the Government in the prescribed form.
  4. Examination. The Government forms a committee which examines the proposal, assessing financial soundness, the sponsoring body's background and the potential of the proposed courses, and reports within about a month, excluding any time spent waiting for information it has asked for.
  5. Letter of Intent. If the Government considers establishment advisable, it issues a Letter of Intent setting out the conditions in clause 9, including the land, covered space and endowment requirements.
  6. Meet the conditions. Secure the thirty acres with clear ownership, construct or arrange the ten thousand square metres of covered space, create the endowment fund, and give the undertakings the guidelines require, then file an affidavit confirming compliance, all within a maximum of one year.
  7. Verification. The Government appoints a committee to verify compliance, which reports within a month. If the conditions are not met within the year, the proposal stands rejected and the Letter of Intent is deemed withdrawn, though the guidelines allow one extension of up to a further year on request.
  8. Establishment by a separate Act. If the Government is satisfied with the verification report, it moves to establish the university. In Rajasthan this is done by the State Legislature passing a dedicated Act for that specific university. The university comes into existence when its own Act is enacted.
  9. Operate, then accredit. The university appoints its statutory officers and faculty, sets up its academic systems, and begins admissions in line with regulator norms, working towards NAAC accreditation under the UGC's requirements.

What you are committing to

The Letter of Intent carries undertakings that shape the academic and financial model, not just the paperwork. Among those in clause 9, the sponsoring body undertakes that:

  • the university will own at least thirty acres of contiguous land and build at least ten thousand square metres of covered space
  • the endowment fund will be created and kept invested
  • books, journals, equipment and infrastructure will be acquired to the minimums set, and scaled up within three and five years
  • at least one Professor and two Readers, with adequate Lecturers and support staff, will be appointed in each department
  • co-curricular and extracurricular activities will be provided, with a provident fund and staff welfare
  • programmes will conform to the norms of the UGC and the concerned statutory bodies

Alongside these, the university will be self-financed and will work towards NAAC accreditation under UGC norms. These are conditions of establishment, not aspirations. A financial model built on fee freedom, on state support, or on affiliating other colleges will not survive contact with them.

Life after establishment

The Act changes what the institution is, but not the conditions it was built under.

A unitary, self-financed university

The university is unitary and self-financed. It cannot affiliate other colleges, and it receives no state grant, so its model rests on its own fees and its sponsoring body's resources.

The endowment stays committed

The endowment fund is created as a security and kept invested, not drawn down as working capital. It is part of the long-term commitment the sponsoring body makes, not recoverable at will.

Accreditation and UGC norms

The university operates under UGC regulations and works towards NAAC accreditation, which is expected early in its life and renewed in cycles. It must comply throughout with UGC and other regulator norms.

Continuing oversight

The university's own Act, together with UGC regulations, sets its reporting and inspection obligations and the State's powers in the event of default. The exact provisions vary with each university's Act, so they should be read in that Act.

How long it takes

There is no guaranteed end to end timeline, and anyone quoting one as a certainty is guessing. What the guidelines fix are a few points:

  • Examination. The committee reports within about a month of its formation, excluding time spent awaiting information.
  • Compliance. The sponsoring body has a maximum of one year to meet the Letter of Intent conditions, extendable once by up to a further year.
  • The Act. The final step depends on the State Legislature passing the university's own Act, so the legislative calendar is a real factor in timing.

Everything else depends on how complete and consistent the file is, and on how ready the land, buildings and funds are when the Letter of Intent arrives. A project that reaches that point with its assets settled moves; one that does not spends its year scrambling to comply.

Where projects go wrong

These are the recurring causes, and nearly all of them are settled long before anything is submitted.

  • Land is not application ready. Held in the wrong name, short of thirty acres, not contiguous, or without clear ownership papers and permissions.
  • The endowment is underestimated. It is capital locked away as a security, not a fee, and the amount depends on location, so it has to be built into the model from the start.
  • The sponsoring body cannot show a financial record. Audited accounts and genuine capacity are part of the file, so a newly formed body with no history struggles even without a formal experience bar.
  • The compliance year is wasted. Treating the Letter of Intent as the finish line, rather than the start of a hard twelve-month build, is how projects lose it.
  • The legislative step is forgotten. Establishment needs the university's own Act passed by the State Legislature, so timing cannot be planned as if it were an administrative signature.
  • Programme approvals are assumed rather than secured. Council approvals for technical, medical or other regulated programmes are separate from the university's own establishment.
  • The file is built on repealed law. The 2005 Act is no longer in force, so a file or an adviser relying on its sections is working from the wrong document.

Questions

Frequently asked questions.

Which law governs private universities in Rajasthan?

Rajasthan passed a general Rajasthan Private Universities Act in 2005, but repealed it with effect from 5 July 2007. Since then the operative framework is the Government of Rajasthan's Guidelines for the Establishment of Private Universities by Separate Act, dated 26 July 2007, and each private university is created by its own dedicated Act of the State Legislature. Because these documents date from 2007, the current position should be confirmed with the Department of College Education, Rajasthan.

How is a private university actually established in Rajasthan?

By its own separate Act. A sponsoring body first secures a Letter of Intent and meets the conditions set in it. The State Government then moves to establish the university, which in Rajasthan is done by the State Legislature passing a dedicated Act for that specific university. The university comes into existence when its own Act is enacted. This is why Rajasthan's statute book carries a separate Act for each private university.

Who can apply to establish a private university in Rajasthan?

Only a sponsoring body. Under clause 1 of the 2007 guidelines that means a society registered under the Rajasthan Societies Registration Act, 1958 or the Societies Registration Act, 1860; a public trust registered under the Rajasthan Public Trusts Act, 1959 or the Indian Trusts Act, 1882; or a company registered under Section 25 of the Companies Act, 1956, now Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013. An individual or a partnership firm cannot apply.

How much land is needed for a private university in Rajasthan?

Under clause 9 of the 2007 guidelines the Letter of Intent conditions require the sponsoring body to own a minimum of 30 acres of contiguous land and to construct a minimum of 10,000 square metres of covered space for administration and academic use. Where UGC or council norms for the programmes offered are higher, those become the effective floor.

How much endowment fund does Rajasthan require?

Under clause 9 of the 2007 guidelines the endowment fund is one crore rupees if the university is at least 15 km from the municipal limits of a Divisional Headquarters city or at least 10 km from other cities, seventy-five lakh rupees if it is in a scheduled tribal area and meets that distance, and two crore rupees in all other cases. The fund is a security the sponsoring body must create and keep invested, not a fee.

What is the Letter of Intent process in Rajasthan?

The sponsoring body applies to the State Government with a proposal, a project report and a demand draft of one lakh rupees to the Commissioner, College Education, Rajasthan. A committee examines the proposal and reports within about a month, excluding time spent waiting for requested information. If the Government considers establishment advisable, it issues a Letter of Intent setting out the conditions in clause 9, which the sponsoring body must meet within a maximum of one year, after which a verification committee reports.

Is NAAC accreditation compulsory for a private university in Rajasthan?

In practice yes. The University Grants Commission's regulations for private universities require accreditation, and each university operates under UGC norms, so NAAC accreditation is expected early in the university's life and renewed in cycles. The exact timeline should be confirmed from the UGC regulations and the university's own Act.

Can a private university in Rajasthan receive government grants?

No. A private university in Rajasthan is self-financed. It is not entitled to a grant or financial assistance from the State Government, so its model has to stand on its own fees and its sponsoring body's resources.

How is Rajasthan different from Madhya Pradesh for establishing a private university?

Rajasthan keeps fixed minimums in its 2007 guidelines, 30 acres of land, 10,000 square metres of covered space and a location-based endowment fund, and creates each university by its own Act of the State Legislature. Madhya Pradesh, after its July 2026 reform, removed the fixed land and endowment minimums and runs approval through a standing Regulatory Commission, establishing universities by adding them to the schedule of a single state Act. The route and the upfront commitment differ materially.

How long does it take to establish a private university in Rajasthan?

There is no guaranteed end to end timeline. The guidelines fix some clocks: the examining committee reports within about a month, and the sponsoring body has up to one year to meet the Letter of Intent conditions, extendable once by up to a year. The final step depends on the State Legislature passing the university's own Act, so the legislative calendar is a real factor. Real time to establishment turns on how complete and consistent the file is.

How to establish a private university in Rajasthan.

Rajasthan sets a clear, front-loaded bar. An eligible sponsoring body applies to the State Government with a project report, a committee examines it, and if the Government is satisfied it issues a Letter of Intent. The sponsoring body then has to put real assets in place, a minimum of thirty acres of land, ten thousand square metres of covered space and an endowment fund, before the university is finally established by a Gazette notification that itself depends on a resolution of the State Legislature. Unlike Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan has kept these fixed minimums, so the capacity to commit land and capital early is part of the test.

By UniMarg Higher Education Consultancy Reviewed by the UniMarg advisory team, October 2026 11 min read

Rajasthan has long been one of the more active states for private universities, and its route is more front-loaded than most. The conditions that decide a project, land, covered space and an endowment fund, have to be met in full before the university comes into existence, and the final step is a legislative one. A promoter who understands this plans the assets and the file together, rather than treating the approval as a licence to be chased.

The law behind it

The governing statute is the Rajasthan Private Universities Act, 2005, read with the state's Guidelines for the Establishment of Private Universities issued in 2007. The Act defines who may sponsor a university, the conditions that attach to a Letter of Intent, the endowment fund, and the way a university is finally notified into existence.

Above the state framework sits the University Grants Commission. Its regulations for private universities require that each is created by a state legislative route, operates as a single unitary university, and that any off campus centre is subject to separate clearance. Under the Act the university is also self-financed, so it cannot look to the State Government for grants.

One caution before the detail. The Act is from 2005 and the guidelines from 2007. Fixed figures of this age are exactly the kind that get revised by amendment or by departmental notification, so every amount on this page should be confirmed against the current gazetted position before it is relied on. The structure of the route, however, has been stable.

Who is allowed to apply

An individual cannot apply, and neither can a partnership firm. The application has to come from a sponsoring body, and under the Act that means one of the following:

  • a society registered under the Rajasthan Societies Registration Act, 1958, or the Societies Registration Act, 1860
  • a public trust registered under the Rajasthan Public Trusts Act, 1959, or the Indian Trusts Act, 1882, or the equivalent law of another state or union territory
  • a company registered under Section 25 of the Companies Act, 1956, now Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013

The project report has to carry the sponsoring body's registration documents, its objectives, its financial resources and its audited accounts for the previous five years, among a long list of required contents. There is no formal bar of prior educational experience, but a sponsoring body with no financial track record will struggle to build a credible file.

Land and buildings

This is where Rajasthan differs most from the states that have relaxed their rules. The Letter of Intent conditions require the sponsoring body to own a minimum of thirty acres of contiguous land, if not already available, and to construct a minimum of ten thousand square metres of covered space for administration and academic purposes.

These are floors, not ceilings. Where the UGC, AICTE or another council sets higher land or infrastructure norms for the programmes an institution intends to run, those norms apply, so a university planning engineering, pharmacy or medical programmes will need the space those disciplines require on top of the basic minimum. The Act also sets minimum early investment in learning resources and equipment, covered below.

The most common and most avoidable problem here is land that is not application ready: held in a promoter's personal name rather than the sponsoring body's, short of the thirty acres, not contiguous, or without clear ownership papers. Each of these is far cheaper to fix a year early than at the compliance stage, when the clock on the Letter of Intent is already running.

What it costs

Application fee

The proposal is filed with the prescribed fee. The 2007 guidelines set this as a demand draft of one lakh rupees in favour of the Commissioner, College Education, Rajasthan, but because fees are prescribed by rule or notification rather than fixed in the Act, the current amount should be confirmed with the Department of College Education.

The endowment fund

Under Section 9 of the Act the sponsoring body must create an endowment fund, and the amount depends on where the university is located.

Location of the universityEndowment fund
At least 15 km from the municipal limits of a Divisional Headquarters city, or at least 10 km from other citiesRs 1 crore
In a scheduled area, and meeting that distance from a cityRs 75 lakh
All other casesRs 2 crore

The logic is deliberate: the state asks for less where a university helps take higher education further from the main cities, and most where it sits in or near them. The fund is not a fee. It is a security deposit that must stay invested until the university is dissolved, its income may be used for infrastructure but not for recurring expenditure, and part or all of it can be forfeited if the university contravenes the Act. In the financial model it is capital locked away for the life of the institution, which is a materially different commitment from a recurring fee.

Early investment in resources and equipment

The Letter of Intent also requires undertakings on early spend: books and journals worth at least ten lakh rupees, with at least fifty lakh rupees invested within three years; and equipment and infrastructure worth at least twenty lakh rupees, rising to at least one crore rupees within five years. Where a regulator's norm is higher, that norm applies.

Rajasthan asks a promoter to put the assets in place first and be established second.

The land, the covered space and the endowment are conditions of the Letter of Intent, met before the university exists. A plan that plans to raise them afterward has the sequence backwards.

The sequence, step by step

In the order it actually happens. The most expensive mistake is starting in the middle, with land or buildings, before the sponsoring body and the file are ready.

  1. Form and test the sponsoring body. Confirm it is one of the permitted registered entities, and that it can show five years of audited accounts and genuine financial capacity. This is a structuring question before it is an application question.
  2. Build the project report. Prepare the full project report the guidelines require: the sponsoring body's registration and finances, the campus location, land and infrastructure, the programmes and faculties proposed with enrolment targets, the fee structure, the admission and staff systems, and the justification for the university.
  3. File the proposal with the State Government. The application, the project report and the prescribed fee go to the Government in the prescribed form.
  4. Examination. The Government forms a committee, including at least one expert, which examines the proposal and reports within about a month, excluding any time spent waiting for information it has asked for.
  5. Letter of Intent. If the Government considers establishment advisable, it issues a Letter of Intent setting out the conditions in Section 5, including the land, covered space and endowment requirements.
  6. Meet the conditions. Secure the thirty acres with clear ownership, construct or arrange the ten thousand square metres of covered space, create the endowment fund, and give the undertakings the Act requires, all within a maximum of one year.
  7. Verification. Submit the compliance report. A verification committee examines it and reports. If the conditions are not met within the year, the proposal stands rejected and the Letter of Intent is deemed withdrawn, though the guidelines allow one extension of up to a further year.
  8. Notification by the state. If satisfied, the State Government establishes the university by notification in the Official Gazette. No such notification may be issued except on a resolution passed by the House of the State Legislature, and the university's name is inserted into the Schedule to the Act.
  9. Operate, then accredit. The university appoints its statutory officers and faculty, sets up its academic systems, and begins admissions in line with regulator norms. It must obtain NAAC accreditation within three years and renew it every five.

What you are committing to

The Letter of Intent carries undertakings that shape the academic and financial model, not just the paperwork. Among them, the sponsoring body undertakes that:

  • the university will own at least thirty acres of contiguous land and build at least ten thousand square metres of covered space
  • the endowment fund will be created and kept invested for the life of the university
  • books, journals, equipment and infrastructure will be acquired to the minimums set, and scaled up within three and five years
  • at least one Professor and two Readers, with adequate Lecturers and support staff, will be appointed in each department
  • programmes will conform to the norms of the UGC and the concerned statutory bodies, and degree standards will be no lower than those set by them
  • the university will be self-financed and will not seek any grant from the State Government
  • NAAC accreditation will be obtained within three years and renewed every five years

These are conditions of establishment, not aspirations. A financial model built on fee freedom, on state support, or on affiliating other colleges will not survive contact with them.

Life after establishment

Notification changes what the institution is, but not the conditions it was built under.

A unitary, self-financed university

The university is unitary and self-financed. It cannot affiliate other colleges, and it receives no state grant, so its model rests on its own fees and its sponsoring body's resources.

The endowment stays locked

The endowment fund remains invested for the life of the university as a security deposit, and can be forfeited in part or whole for contraventions. It is not recoverable working capital.

NAAC accreditation is compulsory

Accreditation by NAAC is required within three years of establishment and must be renewed every five years, and the university must comply throughout with UGC and other regulator norms.

Continuing oversight

The university is subject to the Act's reporting and inspection provisions, and the State retains powers, after due process, to act against a university that defaults, including liquidation in defined circumstances. Dissolution by the sponsoring body itself requires long advance notice.

How long it takes

There is no guaranteed end to end timeline, and anyone quoting one as a certainty is guessing. What the Act and guidelines fix are a few points:

  • Examination. The committee reports within about a month of receiving the proposal, excluding time spent awaiting information.
  • Compliance. The sponsoring body has a maximum of one year to meet the Letter of Intent conditions, extendable once by up to a further year.
  • Notification. The final notification depends on a resolution of the State Legislature, so the legislative calendar is a real factor in timing.

Everything else depends on how complete and consistent the file is, and on how ready the land, buildings and funds are when the Letter of Intent arrives. A project that reaches that point with its assets settled moves; one that does not spends its year scrambling to comply.

Where projects go wrong

These are the recurring causes, and nearly all of them are settled long before anything is submitted.

  • Land is not application ready. Held in the wrong name, short of thirty acres, not contiguous, or without clear ownership papers and permissions.
  • The endowment is underestimated. It is capital locked away for the life of the university, not a fee, and the amount depends on location, so it has to be built into the model from the start.
  • The sponsoring body cannot show a financial record. Five years of audited accounts are part of the file, so a newly formed body with no history struggles even without a formal experience bar.
  • The compliance year is wasted. Treating the Letter of Intent as the finish line, rather than the start of a hard twelve-month build, is how projects lose it.
  • The legislative step is forgotten. Final notification needs a resolution of the State Legislature, so timing cannot be planned as if it were an administrative signature.
  • Programme approvals are assumed rather than secured. Council approvals for technical, medical or other regulated programmes are separate from the university's own establishment.
  • The file is built on dated figures. The Act and guidelines are from 2005 and 2007, so amounts and forms must be checked against the current gazetted position.

Questions

Frequently asked questions.

Which law governs private universities in Rajasthan?

The Rajasthan Private Universities Act, 2005, read with the state's guidelines for the establishment of private universities. The Act sets the conditions, the Letter of Intent route and the role of the State Government and legislature. The Act and the guidelines date from 2005 and 2007, so current figures and forms should be checked against the latest gazetted version and Department of College Education notifications.

Who can apply to establish a private university in Rajasthan?

Only a sponsoring body. Under the Act that means a society registered under the Rajasthan Societies Registration Act, 1958 or the Societies Registration Act, 1860; a public trust registered under the Rajasthan Public Trusts Act, 1959 or the Indian Trusts Act, 1882; or a company registered under Section 25 of the Companies Act, 1956, now Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013. An individual or a partnership firm cannot apply.

How much land is needed for a private university in Rajasthan?

The Letter of Intent conditions require the sponsoring body to own a minimum of 30 acres of contiguous land, if not already available, and to construct a minimum of 10,000 square metres of covered space for administration and academic use. Where UGC or council norms for the programmes offered are higher, those become the effective floor.

How much endowment fund does Rajasthan require?

Under Section 9 of the Act the endowment fund is one crore rupees if the university is at least 15 km from the municipal limits of a Divisional Headquarters city or at least 10 km from other cities, seventy-five lakh rupees if it is in a scheduled area and meets that distance, and two crore rupees in all other cases. The fund is a security deposit that stays invested until the university is dissolved, and part or all of it can be forfeited for contraventions.

How is a private university finally established in Rajasthan?

After the Letter of Intent conditions are met and verified, the State Government establishes the university by notification in the Official Gazette. The Act requires that no such notification is issued except on a resolution passed by the House of the State Legislature, and the university's name is then inserted into the Schedule to the Act.

What is the Letter of Intent process in Rajasthan?

The sponsoring body applies to the State Government with a proposal, a project report and the prescribed fee. A committee that includes at least one expert examines the proposal and reports within about a month, excluding time spent waiting for requested information. If the Government considers establishment advisable, it issues a Letter of Intent setting out the conditions, which the sponsoring body must comply with within a maximum of one year, after which a verification committee reports.

Is NAAC accreditation compulsory for a private university in Rajasthan?

Yes. Under Section 38 of the Act the university must obtain NAAC accreditation within three years of its establishment and renew it every five years, and under Section 39 it is bound to comply with the rules and norms of the UGC and other regulating bodies.

Can a private university in Rajasthan receive government grants?

No. The Act provides that a private university is self-financed and is not entitled to any grant or financial assistance from the State Government. Its financial model has to stand on its own fees and its sponsoring body's resources.

How is Rajasthan different from Madhya Pradesh for establishing a private university?

Rajasthan keeps fixed minimums, 30 acres of land, 10,000 square metres of covered space and an endowment fund, and establishes each university by Gazette notification after a resolution of the State Legislature. Madhya Pradesh, after its July 2026 reform, removed the fixed land and endowment minimums and runs approval through a standing Regulatory Commission. The route and the upfront commitment differ materially.

How long does it take to establish a private university in Rajasthan?

There is no guaranteed end to end timeline. The Act and guidelines fix some clocks: the examining committee reports within about a month, and the sponsoring body has up to one year to comply with the Letter of Intent conditions, extendable once by up to a year. The notification also depends on a resolution of the State Legislature, so the legislative calendar is a factor. Real time to establishment turns on how complete and consistent the file is.
